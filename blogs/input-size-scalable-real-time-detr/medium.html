Toward an Input-Size-Scalable Real-Time DETR

Real-time DETRs are usually designed and benchmarked at 640 × 640. But detecting small objects often calls for high-resolution input. What if the input is 1536 × 864—can we do better than existing state-of-the-art real-time DETRs? This study looks for a DETR that keeps latency low at high resolution, by pairing a lightweight hierarchical backbone and a thinner neck with distillation from a vision foundation model.

The F/8 bottleneck

F/8 is the compute bottleneck: its larger grid has 4× as many positions as F/16, so the same fusion block costs ~500 vs. ~125 GFLOP.

Hypothesis and measurement

Can stronger backbone features reduce the need for an expensive neck? We test two hypotheses:

  1. Distill from a convolutional teacher. DINOv3 ConvNeXt-B may provide better spatially matched supervision for a hierarchical backbone such as MobileNetV4-Hybrid-M.
  2. Make the neck thinner. Stronger features may let us reduce FPN/PAN width from 256 to 192, preserving detection quality while lowering F/8 compute.

Why MobileNetV4?

  • Efficient at high resolution. Our measurements favor a hierarchical CNN backbone at the tested resolution, motivating MobileNetV4 as a candidate for input-size scaling.
  • A natural ConvNeXt student. MobileNetV4's Universal Inverted Bottleneck (UIB) generalizes inverted-bottleneck and ConvNeXt-style blocks. This structural overlap makes it a promising candidate for distillation from DINOv3 ConvNeXt; whether it transfers better remains a hypothesis. MobileNetV4 paper.

Results on COCO val2017

Conclusion

The pre-distilled MobileNetV4 detector roughly ties RT-DETRv4-M on COCO: 52.78 versus 53.29 AP against the baseline's final checkpoint, even with a shorter schedule (best at epoch 69 versus 92) and without a no-augmentation stage. It has 4% fewer parameters and about 16% lower latency. Small objects are the strongest result: 34.94 versus 34.37 APs, even though MobileNetV4's stride-8 feature map has only 80 channels, versus 384 in HGNetv2-B2.

It is also ahead of RF-DETR on small objects at the same size. In RF-DETR's design ablation, the medium model with the DINOv2 backbone reaches 34.3 APs. The next step, to 35.8 APs, comes from additional Objects365 pretraining. That 34.3 already includes 60 epochs of Objects365 pretraining, while our detector uses none. RF-DETR paper, Tables 5 and 6.

Future directions. Distillation still leaves a large gap to the teacher: after 30 epochs, the student's cosine similarity with the teacher is only about 0.55 at F/16 and 0.82 at F/32. A backbone that can absorb more of the teacher's features could raise AP further. On the latency side, stronger backbone features may allow a simpler neck, cutting more of the F/8 fusion cost at high resolution.

Appendix: distillation method

A frozen DINOv3 ConvNeXt-B teacher distills MobileNetV4-Hybrid-M on unlabeled COCO images. In ScaleKD terms, the loss has two parts:

  • Feature mimicking (loss_16, loss_32): projected student features match the teacher's /16 features (standardized per channel) and /32 features.
  • Teacher parameter perception (loss_16r): projected student /16 features pass through the frozen teacher stage 3 and must reproduce the teacher's /32 features.

Each term is 2 − 2·cos per position, with weights 0.5, 1.0 and 0.5. The projectors are discarded after pre-distillation.
